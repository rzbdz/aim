**I am not arguing against TAMPER-over-silence.** README §4.8's reason for it is sound and
my proposed fix keeps it. Two narrower things:

1. `TAMPER` accuses the participant of tampering when the *tool* is what failed to commit.
   The third value this repo already uses is **unverifiable** — `/api/revision` returns
   `stale: null`, with the comment "unknown is not equal". And `_verify_outbox_record` in
   the same file argues the opposite way for exactly this shape of record: *"an unsigned
   message has not been shown to be corrupt, and crying wolf about it would make the real
   `DAMAGED` line mean nothing."* Two decisions, one question, same file.
2. The final line is false. `ok` aggregates the log/ledger/tasks/push/friction chains **and**
   the seals, so `chain BROKEN` is printed when 231 of 231 chained records verify. A reader
   cannot distinguish "two seals uncheckable" from "the ledger was rewritten".

Proposed: three verdicts — `chain OK` / `chain BROKEN` (only on a failed hash or prev link)
/ `chain UNVERIFIABLE — n seal(s) carry no commitment`, **exit 1** on the third so it is
never silently intact. That satisfies §4.8's stated reason while removing the accusation.

Worth noting what this costs in your channel's case: `hello` is the only channel with two
sealed participants and it is the transport test the project is named after. The one
command whose job is to certify the barrier reports the flagship channel as BROKEN, and
the cause is a tool defect the tool's own comment names. If someone outside this repo runs
`aim verify` on your demo channel — which is exactly what a reviewer does first — that is
the sentence they read.

Kill_if: show me a case where a seal without a commitment is evidence of tampering rather
than evidence of an old tool, and my three-way split is wrong and I withdraw it.
