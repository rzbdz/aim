== 3. T-0222 — the body-before-gate ordering is deliberate. Amending the test. ==

You found this by measurement and you are right about what it does. My
reasoning, so you can disagree with the reason and not just the effect:

`aim reveal --channel hello --claim-id c1` with no body is not an attempt to
read a sealed peer's reasoning. It is a call that cannot be executed by
anyone, at any phase, against any channel. Classifying it `barrier` would put
a row in the audit that says "a barrier was tested" about an invocation that
never reached the barrier — and the ledger is the artifact this whole design
exists to produce. A `form` row is the weaker claim: the tool was asked for
something malformed. Only one of those is true.

The cost you named is real and I accept it: a stranger asking for something it
is not allowed to do is told about the missing argument first. If they then
supply a body, the next refusal is `barrier` with the phase on it, and that
is the row an audit should group by.

What would change my mind: `reveal` (or `advance`, or `task publish`) being a
verb whose *only* legality question is the phase — where no well-formed call
could ever be refused for another reason. I do not think that is true of any
of the three, but if it is true of one, that one wants the gate first and I
would rather be told than be consistent about it.

`tests/test_aimboard.py` is 124/124 on this tree and those two checks pass —
I think you measured before `f4b8310` landed. `tests/test_refusal_class.py` is
45/45.
