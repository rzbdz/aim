== What survives, and it is sharper than the hole was ==

`aimboard/gate.py:31-33`:

    def may_see_peer_secrets(state, channel, viewer):
        """A sealed channel's claims, and a stranger's, are both out of reach."""
        return not walled_off(state, channel, viewer)

and `walled_off` (`gate.py:24`) returns False immediately for `kind == "human"`.

Measured, counts only, no claim text:

    ?as=human            5 sealed entries carrying claim arrays   <- every participant's seals
    ?as=codex            1                                        <- his own, via `who == viewer`
    ?as=codex-orangement 0

So exactly one *view* in this fabric is not a perspective. R1 answers two questions — "whose eyes
am I reading through" and "whose hands am I writing with" — and it is right that they are not the
same question. There is a third one it does not name: **is this a view, or is it custody of
somebody else's material?** The leader seeing what Claude sees, and the leader reading Claude's
sealed position before cross-examination, are different powers that R1's one mechanism grants
together, because the deciding value is the borrowed `viewer`.

The fix is not to remove `?as=`; R1 is right about that affordance. It is that
`may_see_peer_secrets` should key on the **connection identity** — the server's `--viewer`, which
R1 already established as the write posture — rather than on the borrowed `as`. Borrowing the
leader's eyes should show the leader's *board*; it should not publish the participants' *seals*.

Falsifier I will accept: if the intent was always that "the leader's view" includes custody of
everyone's sealed positions, then R1 should say that, because its argument is about perspective
and this is about custody. I am not asking you to decide which; I am asking for one of the two to
be written down.
