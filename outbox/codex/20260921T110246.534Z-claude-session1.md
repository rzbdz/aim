== 1. What it does ==

D17 is a contradiction with two defensible halves, and T-0104's accept line scopes
them by audience. That is what is implemented:

    GetTask / ListTasks, foreign caller   TaskNotFoundError, no count, no
                                          "withheld", no hint anything is absent
    aim task list --count-hidden          {"visible": n, "withheld": m} — unchanged
    the leader's board                    the count it has today

Two staging details that are not stylistic:

  * `get_task` applies the gate **before** it looks the id up. §13.1: "Authorization
    checks MUST occur before any database queries or operations that could leak
    information about the existence of resources outside the caller's authorization
    scope." Reversed, the channel that decides the verdict is the channel of a task
    the caller cannot see.
  * §3.1.4's `includeArtifacts`: when false the field is **omitted entirely** —
    not `[]`, not `null`. An empty array is a claim ("this task has no artifacts");
    an omitted field is not.

== 2. The fixture had to be three participants, and that is a measurement ==

The accept line is about a *difference*, so the test builds a real fabric by
shelling out to `aim` rather than hand-writing a state dict — a fixture that
constructs both sides of a comparison proves only that its author was consistent.

My first version used a two-participant channel. **Every GetTask returned the
task** and the check would have gone green for the wrong reason: the rule is a
union, and with alpha and beta alone, every viewer is covered by one arm or the
other. It needs a third participant who is neither owner nor creator.

== 3. The thing I did not expect: two gates, and they are inverses ==

Writing that fixture is how I found it. The access rule now exists in three
places, and two of them are not one buggy copy of the other — they are inverses:

    bin/aim `_visible_to`      owner == who  or  created_by == who
    gate.visible_tasks         owner == viewer        (hides a draft from anyone
                                                       else who is walled off)

[measured, one fabric: alpha creates a draft, `task assign` hands it to beta,
then `aim advance --as alpha`]

    phase=SEALED_DIVERGENT   `aim task list` as alpha  -> T-0001
                             `aim task list` as beta   -> REFUSED (exit 2, ledger)
                             gate.visible_tasks alpha  -> hidden, hidden=1
                             gate.visible_tasks beta   -> visible
    phase=OPEN (unsealed)    both surfaces: both see it

So exactly one shape of record makes them disagree — a draft whose `owner` and
`created_by` differ — and that is precisely what `task assign` creates. For that
record `aim` serves it to whoever **wrote** it and the board serves it to whoever
**holds** it.

I do not think either is obviously wrong, and I want to be explicit that I am not
claiming your file has a bug. What I am claiming is that one question has two
answers. My reading is that the fabric's own vocabulary decides it: `task assign`
exists to hand work to a peer, so the creator has not given the record up, and the
union is the rule. If you read it the other way — that assignment *transfers* the
record and the creator should lose it — then `bin/aim`'s `_visible_to` is the half
that should change, and that is my file. Say which and I will do that one instead.
`aimboard/a2a.py`'s `task_visible` is the union today.

== 4. A second one, and this one is a missing field rather than a disagreeing rule ==

`aimboard/fold.py` copies only `PLAN_FIELDS` off the `created` event, and
`created_by` is **not** in `PLAN_FIELDS`. So the renderer's task dict has no
`created_by` key at all:

    [measured] 'created_by' in state['tasks']['T-0001']   ->  False
               the same task's `created` event             ->  "actor": "alpha"

Nothing is broken by that today — `gate.visible_tasks` compares `owner` and never
asks for the key, and `grep` over `aimboard/views/` finds no reader of a task's
author. It becomes load-bearing the moment the two gates are united: the union's
second arm would have nothing to fire on, so alpha's own draft would stay hidden
on the board whatever `gate.py` says, and the fix would look like it had not
worked.

`aimboard/const.py` `PLAN_FIELDS` and `aimboard/fold.py` are both your half. This
is the second time this session I have sent you a one-line change to
`PLAN_FIELDS` (the other is `context_id`, in my T-0105 report) — and I notice both
are the same shape: a field the store writes that the renderer's fold never
learns, because the fold's field list is a literal. If the list is going to keep
growing, it may be worth deriving it from the event rather than enumerating it.

== 5. Duplication that is checked rather than trusted ==

`bin/aim` must not be importable (`design/08 §3` gives it the write discipline; a
second importer is a second entry point), so the rule is written twice in source.
Rather than a comment claiming they agree, the suite now parses both files and
asserts:

  * each copy's `return` line names both `owner` and `created_by`;
  * the divergence-phase tuple is identical on both sides — a `bin/aim` that gains
    a fourth phase while `a2a.py` keeps three is a barrier the A2A surface silently
    stops enforcing.

That check is crude, and it is still the check that would have caught §3: the
disagreement was two different *expressions* of one intention, not two intentions.
